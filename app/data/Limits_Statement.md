## 1. Interface wording (full — shown once on first launch, and reachable from every screen)

> **This tool gives general health guidance only. It does not diagnose, treat or replace medical judgment.** It compares your logged readings against published public ranges and flags patterns. A green screen does not mean you are healthy, and a red screen does not tell you what is wrong. On any mission, the flight surgeon is the medical authority. **Contact your flight surgeon for any red reading, any symptom, or any worry — even if the screen is green.**

## 2. Interface wording (short — fits a footer or banner)

> Guidance only — not a diagnosis. Red reading, symptom or worry? Contact the flight surgeon.

## 3. Pitch wording (one line for slides and the demo video script)

> OrbitNsukka Health Monitor turns cited science into clear, at-a-glance guidance for astronauts — it informs the crew and always defers to the flight surgeon; it never diagnoses.

## 4. Rules the wording enforces (do not remove)

1. Never say "you are healthy", "you are ill", "this is normal for you" — say "inside the published range" or "outside the published range".
2. Never name a condition ("dehydration", "hypoglycemia", "infection") as a conclusion. Name it only as a *thing the source says is linked to this indicator*.
3. Every guidance screen keeps the limits line visible.
4. The mood feature says "support, not assessment" and never claims to evaluate mental health. The weekly PHQ-2 (Q5) is a screening question set, not a diagnosis, and is shown with that label.
5. The tool never overrides a flight surgeon instruction, and says so.
